GENERAL SYNTHESIS FOR THE PAPER
Services remain robust and stable. Across both C5 tests, 6/8 systems reached F1 = 1.0000 in services. The two exceptions are the same as in C1: Cargo-Tracker (missing implicit service in the reference) and TNTConcept (missing implicit Logistics Network). Consolidation without metrics did not change service F1 in 7/8 systems in Test 1, and in 8/8 systems in Test 2. The single exception was Cargo-Tracker in Test 1, where the consolidator followed the proposal with lower service agreement — a choice that the reference-informed configuration would likely have corrected.

Interactions are the main locus of instability. In the reference-free configuration, the consolidator's choice is unstable across runs for the only systems where A and B genuinely differ (Cargo-Tracker and, marginally, TNTConcept). In Test 1, A3 chose B in Cargo-Tracker (interactions 0.6087); in Test 2, A3 chose A in the same system (interactions 0.0000). This run-to-run divergence is the clearest empirical signature of what is lost when A3 has no metric signal.

A3 is a double-edged sword in the reference-free setting. In Test 1, it made a defensible but unverifiable choice (better interactions, worse services). In Test 2, it made a clearly inferior choice (interactions 0.0000 when B offered 0.5263) and produced corrupted YAML. The reference-free configuration therefore adds a failure mode that did not appear with the same frequency in the metric-conditioned C1 runs.

Cost profile differs from C1. Agent 2 accounts for ~34% of tokens in both C5 tests, well below the ~46% observed in the C1 runs. This is because Agent 3 consumed more tokens per run (especially in Test 1), likely due to retries and longer outputs in the absence of a converging score. Total cost is high (≈520k–540k tokens per run) and roughly comparable to the C1 runs, confirming that removing the reference from A3 does not reduce cost; it only removes its ability to discriminate.

Observability and YAML limitations. The tracker still does not separate prompt tokens, labels Agent 5 as unknown, and records anomalous behavior for Agent 3 (multiple retries, corrupted output in Cargo-Tracker Test 2). YAML output is generally consistent, but the Cargo-Tracker Test 2 corruption is a concrete limitation that propagates from A3 to Agent 5 and should be reported.

Requirements remain the dominant factor. The two systems with imperfect reference alignment (Cargo-Tracker and TNTConcept) are the only ones where configuration choices matter. In the other six systems, A and B converge, and the reference-free consolidator has no room to differentiate itself. This reinforces the broader finding of the paper: the ceiling is set by the requirements and by the reference itself, not by the orchestration.

Scope statement for the paper. The C5 results should be reported as: verified on Gemini, where the C1–C3 and C2–C3 contrasts for interactions were significant. In the reference-free configuration, the consolidator fails to recover the better candidate in the one system where the difference is largest (Cargo-Tracker Test 2), and its choices are not stable across runs. This supports the paper's argument that consolidation gains are conditioned on the availability of the reference, and that reference-free consolidation is an open design problem — not a solved one.
